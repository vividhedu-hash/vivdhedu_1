3.1 Mathematical Course Match & Relevance Formulation

Every candidate course \(c\) evaluated for student \(s\) must surpass a rigorous Match Threshold score of \(\mathcal{M}(s, c) \ge 0.75\) before entering the recommendation pool:

Equation 3.1 — Dynamic Course Match Scoring Tensor
$$ \mathcal{M}(s, c) = w_1\cdot\Delta_{\text{skill}}(s,c) + w_2\cdot\text{Align}_{\text{career}}(s,c) + w_3\cdot\rho_{\text{AI}}(c) + w_4\cdot\mathcal{B}_{\text{fit}}(s,c) $$
Breaking Down the Four Core Factors in Plain Terms:
  • \(\Delta_{\text{skill}}(s,c) = 1 - \frac{|\mathbf{k}_s \cap \mathbf{k}_c|}{|\mathbf{k}_c|}\): The Missing Skill Factor. Checks what percentage of the course curriculum teaches skills you currently lack. If a course spends 80% of its time on things you already know, its score is heavily penalized.
  • \(\text{Align}_{\text{career}}(s,c) = \cos\left(\mathbf{v}_{\text{target}}(s),\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{syllabus}}(c)\right)\): Career Goal Alignment. Measures how closely the skills taught in the course match the real-world requirements of your dream 10-year job.
  • \(\rho_{\text{AI}}(c) = \sum_{j \in \mathbf{k}_c} \text{ResilienceScore}(j)\cdot \text{Weight}(j)\): The AI Defense Factor. Rewards courses that teach deep systems thinking, complex problem-solving, and hands-on execution, while downranking courses that only teach routine syntax or rote formulas that AI can easily do.
  • \(\mathcal{B}_{\text{fit}}(s,c) = \max\!\left(0,\, 1 - \frac{\text{Price}_{\text{INR}}(c)}{\text{DiscretionaryMonthlyBudget}(s)}\right)\): Budget Accessibility. Prevents the system from recommending overpriced courses to students with modest family budgets, steering them instead toward high-yield certifications, financial aid, or free open-source equivalents.
  • Factor Weights: Initialized to: \(w_1 = 0.35\) (Skills needed), \(w_2 = 0.25\) (Career fit), \(w_3 = 0.25\) (AI safety), \(w_4 = 0.15\) (Budget fit), summing to 1.0.

Section 04 · Econometric Modeling

The Student Asset Pricing Engine: Complete Mathematical Framework

The core philosophy of IndiaLens is that every college degree or career pathway is a multi-decade financial investment. Instead of relying on misleading college brochures that quote cherry-picked starting salaries, IndiaLens models your real future earnings using probability-adjusted income taxes, loan repayment schedules, economic recessions, and multi-year simulations.

💡 In Plain English · The Big Question We Answer

"If my family invests ₹20 Lakhs in this college today, how much real money will I actually take home over the next 20 years?"
Most college counselors only look at the highest salary on a brochure. IndiaLens calculates your real take-home cash. We subtract taxes, deduct monthly loan EMIs, adjust for inflation (rising living costs), simulate the chance of recessions or layoffs, calculate whether AI will replace that job, and if studying abroad, calculate visa lottery odds and currency exchange rates. This gives you one clear rupee amount: is this college an investment that makes you financially secure, or an overpriced trap?

4.1 The Master Net Present Value (NPV) Formulation

The 20-Year Net Present Value represents the total real-world wealth generated by your college education compared to working right away after 12th grade:

Equation 4.1 — Multi-Dimensional Global Net Present Value
$$ \text{NPV}(s,p) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\hat{S}(s,p,t)\cdot\left(1 - \tau(y_t,\kappa)\right)\cdot P_{\text{visa}}(t,d)\cdot\text{FX}(t,\text{curr})\cdot\left(1 - P_{\text{rec}}(t)\cdot \chi_{\text{loss}}\right)\cdot\left(1 - \delta_{\text{AI}}(p,t)\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{t}\left(1 + r(k)\right)} - \mathcal{C}_{\text{invest}}(p,s) - \text{OC}_{\text{PLFS}}(e,\sigma)\cdot d_{\text{years}} $$
Understanding Every Part of This Equation in Plain Terms:
  • \(\hat{S}(s,p,t)\): Expected Gross Salary. Your realistic predicted salary in year \(t\) (from year 1 to 20), calculated by looking at thousands of past graduates across the entire salary spectrum—from 10th percentile to 90th percentile—rather than just the top 1% placed on the brochure.
  • \(\tau(y_t,\kappa)\): Income Taxes. What the government takes away. In India, it uses the official New Tax Regime slabs (escalating to 30% plus cess); in the US, federal, state (e.g. 0% in Texas vs 13.3% in California), and FICA taxes; in Germany, the progressive income tax.
  • \(P_{\text{visa}}(t,d)\): Visa & Work Authorization Odds. For domestic Indian colleges, this is 100% (1.0). For foreign degrees, it calculates the realistic probability of winning the US H-1B visa lottery, securing a UK Graduate Route extension, or getting an EU Blue Card.
  • \(\text{FX}(t,\text{curr})\): Currency Swings (Exchange Rate). If you study abroad and earn dollars, pounds, or euros, this models how changing currency rates affect what your savings are worth when sending money home to India.
  • \(P_{\text{rec}}(t)\cdot \chi_{\text{loss}}\): Recession & Layoff Factor. Accounts for the historical reality that an economic downturn or hiring freeze occurs roughly once every 10–12 years, causing temporary pay cuts or job search delays.
  • \(\delta_{\text{AI}}(p,t)\): AI Automation Drag. The gradual loss of salary bargaining power as automated AI tools take over routine job tasks.
  • \(r(k)\): Time Value of Money (Discount Rate). A rupee earned 15 years from now buys less than a rupee today because of inflation and interest rates.
  • \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{invest}}(p,s)\): Total True Cost of College. Everything you pay out of pocket: tuition fees, hostel, food, coaching, textbooks, laptop, and travel across all study years.
  • \(\text{OC}_{\text{PLFS}}(e,\sigma)\): Opportunity Cost. The baseline income you could have earned if you started working right away after Class 12 instead of spending 4 years in college.

4.2 Internal Rate of Return (IRR) Formulation

The Internal Rate of Return represents the annual compounding growth rate of the money you invest in your education:

💡 In Plain English · What IRR Means for Parents

Think of college like a bank fixed deposit or mutual fund. If an education loan charges 11% annual interest, but your degree only delivers an IRR of 7%, you are losing money every single month. IndiaLens warns you whenever a degree's return is lower than your education loan interest rate.

Equation 4.2 — Internal Rate of Return via Newton-Raphson Iteration
$$ r^* \quad \text{such that} \quad \text{NPV}(s,p \mid r^*) = 0 $$ $$ r^*_{n+1} = r^*_n - \frac{\text{NPV}(r^*_n)}{\left.\frac{\partial \text{NPV}}{\partial r}\right|_{r^*_n}} = r^*_n + \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\mathcal{F}(t)}{(1+r^*_n)^t} - \mathcal{C}_{\text{total}}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{t\cdot\mathcal{F}(t)}{(1+r^*_n)^{t+1}}} $$
Where \(\mathcal{F}(t)\) denotes your net take-home cash flow in year \(t\), and \(\mathcal{C}_{\text{total}}\) is your total college cost plus forgone earnings. If \(r^* < r_{\text{loan}}\) (your education loan interest rate), the degree destroys family wealth.

Instead of assuming everything in life goes perfectly, our computer simulates 10,000 different possible life paths for you. What if there is an economic recession the year you graduate? What if bank loan interest rates go up by 2%? What if you take 4 extra months to find a job? By testing your loan against 10,000 randomized futures, IndiaLens calculates the exact percentage chance your family will experience loan repayment distress—and warns you before you sign any bank loan papers.

Equation 4.4 — Stochastic Loan Stress & Default Probability Engine
$$ \text{EMI}_i(t) = \frac{P_{\text{principal}}\cdot r_m(i,t)\cdot\left(1 + r_m(i,t)\right)^n}{\left(1 + r_m(i,t)\right)^n - 1} \cdot \left(1 + \xi_{\text{MCLR}}(i,t)\right) $$ $$ d\xi_{\text{MCLR}} = \kappa\left(\bar{\xi} - \xi_{\text{MCLR}}\right)dt + \sigma_{\text{MCLR}} dW_t \quad \text{(Floating Bank Rate Drift)} $$ $$ \hat{S}_i(t) = \hat{S}(t)\cdot\left(1 + \varepsilon_{\text{vol},i}\right)\cdot\left(1 - \delta_{\text{AI}}(t)\right)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\text{NoRecession}_i(t)\}} + \hat{S}(t)\cdot(1-\chi_{\text{loss}})\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\text{Recession}_i(t)\}} $$ $$ \text{DefaultEvent}_i = \mathbf{1}\left[\exists\, t \in [1, n] : \text{EMI}_i(t) > \omega_{\text{distress}}\cdot\frac{\hat{S}_i(t)}{12}\cdot\left(1 - \tau_{\text{eff}}\right)\cdot\left(1 - r_{\text{PF}}\right)\right] $$ $$ \Pr[\text{Default}] = \left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N=10{,}000} \text{DefaultEvent}_i\right) \times 100\% $$ $$ T_{\text{BEP}}^{50} = \text{median}\left\{\min\left\{t \ge 1 : \sum_{k=1}^{t}\left(\frac{\hat{S}_i(k)}{12}\cdot s_{\text{rate}} - \text{EMI}_i(k)\right) \ge \mathcal{C}_{\text{invest}}(p,s)\right\}\right\} $$
Key Elements of the Simulation:
  • \(\text{DefaultEvent}_i\): Triggered whenever your monthly loan EMI exceeds 38% of your net monthly take-home salary (after taxes and mandatory Provident Fund deductions).
  • \(\Pr[\text{Default}]\): The proportion of the 10,000 simulated life paths where the student faces loan distress. Below 5% is Safe; 5%–15% is Moderate Risk; above 20% triggers a High Risk warning.
  • \(T_{\text{BEP}}^{50}\): Median Breakeven Period—the exact number of months it will take for your accumulated net savings to fully repay your college education costs.

5.1 Task-Level S-Curve Automation Decay Engine

IndiaLens models automation using an empirical S-curve diffusion formula that tracks how quickly companies adopt AI tools for specific tasks:

Equation 5.1 — Task Automation S-Curve Decay Function
$$ \delta_{\text{AI}}(p,t) = D_0(p)\cdot\left(1 - H(p)\right)\cdot\left(1 - \exp\!\left(-\lambda_{\text{AI}}\cdot t\right)\right)\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\text{adopt}}(p,t) $$ $$ \mathcal{M}_{\text{adopt}}(p,t) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\left(-k_p\left(t - t_{0,p}\right)\right)} \quad \text{(Sector-Specific Adoption Envelope)} $$
Understanding the Terms in Plain English:

6.1 The 3-Parameter Logistic (3PL) Mathematical Model

The probability that a student \(j\) with underlying strength level \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_j\) selects the optimal answer on question \(i\) is modeled mathematically as:

Equation 6.1 — 3PL Item Response Probability Function
$$ P(x_{ij} = 1 \mid \boldsymbol{\theta}_j, a_i, b_i, c_i) = c_i + \frac{1 - c_i}{1 + \exp\!\left(-a_i\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}_j - b_i\right)\right)} $$
Understanding the Three Parameters in Simple Terms:
  • \(a_i > 0\): Discrimination Power. How sharply this specific question separates students with high aptitude from students who are struggling. High-\(a\) questions give maximum diagnostic insight.
  • \(b_i \in [-3.0, +3.0]\): Question Difficulty. How tough the question is. An average student has a 50% chance of solving a question with difficulty \(b_i\).
  • \(c_i \in [0, 0.25]\): Lucky Guessing Protection. Accounts for students who randomly pick an answer or make a careless slip, ensuring a single mistake does not unfairly hurt their overall score.

6.2 Fisher Information Maximization & Dynamic Item Selection

Rather than giving every student the exact same fixed set of questions, the test dynamically selects whichever next question provides the highest diagnostic certainty:

Equation 6.2 — Fisher Information & Optimal Item Selection
$$ \mathbf{I}_i(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = a_i^2 \cdot \frac{P_i(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - c_i}{(1 - c_i)^2} \cdot \frac{\left(1 - P_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)}{P_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})} \cdot \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{u}_i^T $$ $$ \text{Item}_{t+1} = \arg\max_{i \in \text{Unadministered}} \det\!\left(\sum_{k \in \text{Administered}} \mathbf{I}_k(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_t) + \mathbf{I}_i(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_t)\right) $$
This mathematical selection rule guarantees that every single question presented extracts the maximum possible insight about your natural instincts and career preferences.

6.3 Expected A-Posteriori (EAP) Trait Updates

After each answer you give, IndiaLens instantly updates your profile using Bayesian mathematics:

Equation 6.3 — Gauss-Hermite Quadrature Trait Estimation & Stopping Rule
$$ \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\text{EAP}} = \frac{\sum_{q=1}^{41} \mathbf{X}_q \cdot \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{1:t} \mid \mathbf{X}_q) \cdot \mathbf{W}_q}{\sum_{q=1}^{41} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{1:t} \mid \mathbf{X}_q) \cdot \mathbf{W}_q} \quad \text{where} \quad \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{1:t} \mid \mathbf{X}_q) = \prod_{k=1}^{t} P_k(\mathbf{X}_q)^{x_k} \left(1 - P_k(\mathbf{X}_q)\right)^{1 - x_k} $$ $$ \text{Standard Error: } \text{SE}(\hat{\theta}_d) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{t} \mathbf{I}_{k,d}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}} $$ $$ \text{Diagnostic Termination Criterion: } \max_{d \in \{1,2,3,4\}} \text{SE}(\hat{\theta}_d) < 0.35 \quad \text{OR} \quad n_{\text{items}} \ge 8 $$
